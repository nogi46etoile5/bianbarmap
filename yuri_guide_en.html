📜 The History & Cultural Evolution of Yuri, GL, and WLW Fiction

The genre of Yuri and sapphic fiction has evolved significantly through Japanese literary history. Explore its journey from Meiji-era "Class S" culture to modern global GL and WLW media.
1. Meiji & Taisho: Spiritual Purity and the Aesthetic of "Class S"

Represented by Nobuko Yoshiya's "Hana Monogatari" (Flower Stories), the "Class S" culture was a realm of pure, pseudo-sisterly affection permitted to schoolgirls under patriarchal pressures before marriage. Its spiritual nobility and fleeting beauty remain a core aesthetic in modern Yuri and WLW fiction.

2. 1970s: Dramatic Art and the Impact of the "Year 24 Group"

In the 1970s, the emergence of the "Year 24 Group" (including Moto Hagio and Keiko Takemiya) revolutionized shojo manga. Romantic feelings between women evolved beyond friendship into dramatic art that questioned human solitude, existence, and spiritual longing.

3. 2000s: Stylistic Beauty and the "Sanctuary"

"Maria Watches Over Us" established iconic tropes like all-girls academies and the "soeur" system. Yuri became its own specialized domain, fostering a dedicated sanctuary with the launch of magazines like Comic Yuri Hime.

4. 2010s & Beyond: Explosion of Diversity, Sapphic Romance & Modern WLW

Expanding beyond school settings into adult workplace drama, sci-fi, fantasy, and social issues, Yuri has evolved into globally resonant stories of mutual solidarity, sapphic romance, and personal commitment.
