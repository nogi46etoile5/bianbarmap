Basic rules and etiquette you should know before visiting Lesbian Bars and MIX Bars

01What is a Lesbian Bar (Bian Bar)?

These are bars exclusively for women (cisgender women, transgender women, queer women, etc.). While internationally known as "Lesbian Bars," in local Japanese conversations and social media, they are widely referred to using the abbreviation/slang "Bian Bar." Male guests are not permitted, creating a safe space for women to enjoy conversations and drinks together.

💡 Entry policies for FTM (Transgender Men) vary by establishment. We recommend checking in advance using our filter search or on individual bar detail pages.

02What is a MIX Bar?

A MIX bar is an establishment open to everyone regardless of gender, sexuality, or nationality. They are ideal if you are visiting with male friends, in a mixed group, or travelling.

Perfect for those who want to enjoy drinks in an open, LGBTQ+ friendly atmosphere.

03Payment Systems

Nightlife bars in Japan primarily use two payment methods:

  • Cash on Delivery (Cash on): Paying for each drink or food item immediately as it is served. This makes tracking your budget easy and prevents overspending.
  • Tab System (Post-payment / Check): You are seated upon entering and settle your bill all at once when leaving, similar to standard restaurants.

04Table Cover Charge & Ordering Rules

Most bars charge a "Table Cover Charge" (seat fee) of around 1,000 to 2,000 JPY per person upon entry. *Note that unlike Japanese izakayas, complimentary appetizers (Otoshi) are often not served.

Even if there is no explicit time limit, ordering **at least one drink per hour** is considered polite etiquette for a smooth experience.

05How Bottle Keep Works

A system where you purchase a whole bottle of liquor (like shochu or whisky) which the bar stores for you (**typically held for 3 months**).

  • If you plan to visit regularly, this is significantly cheaper than ordering individual shots or glasses each time.
  • On subsequent visits, you only pay the "Set Fee" (covering ice, mixers, and table charge).

06Essential Bar Terms & Recommended Drinks

  • Mama / Manager / Owner: Terms for the person running the bar. While "Mama" is standard in gay bars, lesbian bars often use "Manager" (Tencho), "Owner", or their actual name.
  • Miseko (Staff): Staff members working behind the counter serving drinks and chatting with guests.
  • Shot: A small glass of high-proof liquor meant to be consumed quickly.
  • Staff Drink (Cast Drink): Buying a drink for staff members as a gesture of appreciation or to celebrate together.
  • Popular Drinks: Check our note article for recommended drinks and tips on how to order smoothly.

  • O-aiso (Check/Bill): Settle the bill. In loud bars, making an "X" sign with your index fingers is the universal signal for asking for the check.
  • Mixers (Set): Water, soda water, green tea, or oolong tea used when drinking from your kept bottle.

07Important Etiquette Before Entering

  • Bring Cash: Even if cards are accepted, terminal errors or fees can happen, so having Japanese Yen cash on hand is safest.
  • Travel Light: Bars in Shinjuku Ni-chome are often small and compact. Avoid bringing large suitcases or bulky luggage into the venue.
  • Always Ask Before Taking Photos: To protect the privacy of customers and staff, taking photos/videos inside without permission or posting them to social media is strictly prohibited.
  • Respect Personal Space: Getting overly drunk, excessive physical touch, or forcing people to exchange contact info is bad etiquette. Maintain a comfortable personal space for everyone.

08Ni-chome Culture & Local Terminology

Common local terms and gender/style categories used in Shinjuku Ni-chome and the local community.

  • Ni-chome (Nichou) / Bian: Abbreviation for "Shinjuku Ni-chome" and a common local term for Lesbian (Bian).
  • Tachi / Neko / Riva: Roles/preferences in relationships (Tachi = lead/top role; Neko = receptive/bottom role; Riva = switch/either).
  • Femme / Chusei / Boyi (Boyish): Classifications based on presentation and fashion style.
    • Femme: Feminine and stylish aesthetic.
    • Chusei (Androgynous/Neutral): Soft, cool style that blurs gender lines.
    • Boyi (Boyish): Short hair, tomboyish, or casual masculine style.
🌏 Note for International Guests:
In Japanese culture, "Boyi" and "Chusei" differ somewhat from Western terms like "Butch" or "Androgynous." They generally refer to a soft, smart, and neutral street fashion style influenced by Japanese subculture rather than a strict masculine presentation.
